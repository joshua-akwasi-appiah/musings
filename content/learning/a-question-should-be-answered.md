---
title: A Question Should Be Answered
description: What a dismissive reply to an obvious question can teach us about confidence, curiosity and learning.
---

Have you ever had someone tell you, quite nicely, during a conversation, that you have asked a dumb, stupid, senseless or useless question?

Well, I have. Quite a number of times, actually.

I guess if you like asking questions as much as I do, it is bound to happen at some point. As many times as it has happened, though, you would think I would be “desensitised” to it by now. But no. Somehow, it leaves me feeling upset every time it happens.

And today’s episode had me fuming.

I mean, this friend, let’s call her Janice, because it is a nice name and I do not have a friend called Janice 😂, shared a challenge she was having with me. In response, I asked a fairly obvious question, quite frankly.

I expected a yes or no. That would have led me to reveal the thinking behind the question, which could very well have turned into an idea.

But no. Janice had to pleasantly tell me I had asked a dumb question.

Did she have to say that? I do not think so.

And so, I launched into attack mode. Yes, I had asked the dumb question. And yes, I felt justified in attacking her. Because what do you mean I have asked a dumb question? 😂

Long story short, Janice understood why I had asked the question, apologised for saying it was dumb, and begged me to share the thinking behind it. Yes, she had to beg me. 😌

This incident led me to reflect on why I got so upset when Janice called me out for asking a dumb question.

I realised that when we “condemn” people for asking certain questions, we indirectly tell them that it is not always safe to ask when they do not understand something.

Especially among children and young people, we can create a society where they do not feel allowed to ask their questions. So they carry their ignorance forward. In the end, that can kill their curiosity and hurt both them and society at large.

So, my message here is simple: when someone asks a stupid, obvious or useless question, just be gracious and answer.

They may genuinely not know. Or they may be building towards an idea. The way you respond could spur them on or shut them down.

Of course, there will be instances where people ask questions that are too personal or, quite frankly, rude. Well, that is a story for another day.

But for today, the message is simply this: answer when someone asks.

Anyway, that brings me to the end of my musings for today. I am interested in hearing your thoughts.

Have you ever had someone subtly hint that you asked a dumb question? How did it feel?

And have you ever been the one shutting others down because they asked a useless question? In that case, in Jesus’ words, go and sin no more. 😄
