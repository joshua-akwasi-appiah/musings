---
title: "But I Used the Juice: The Dunning-Kruger Effect"
description: A conversation about an old dream, confidence, caution and the Dunning-Kruger effect.
---

Have you ever been in a situation where someone encouraged you to do something you genuinely knew you could do, but you still found yourself seized with fear? Or have you ever hesitated to do something you knew you were capable of?

Well, this was me yesterday.

Funny enough, it happened during a conversation with a friend. Let’s call her Morgana (because at one point I thought it was the sweetest name ever 😄).

I had been explaining to her something I had read recently and how it had changed my perspective on the subject. When I finished, she said something along the lines of, “Wow, you should write a book.”

I burst out laughing because I found it funny.

But she said, “No, seriously. Seriously. I believe you are creative and that you could actually be a writer.”

You would think I would have met her encouragement with excitement.

But no.

I met it with lots of excuses. I kept pouring water on the fire she was trying to ignite in me.

Now, before you start pointing fingers, in my defence, I had not thought seriously about being a writer in years. At one point in my life, it was one of my childhood dreams. I imagined myself publishing books and experiencing the excitement and fulfilment that would come with finally doing it.

But then I grew up. I saw more of life. I began to understand that writing a book is not as simple as having an idea and putting words on a page. And somehow, somewhere along the way, I stopped thinking about it altogether.

Thankfully, Morgana did not leave it at that. She explained why she thought I could do it. She had seen how I communicate, how I explain concepts and ideas, and how I break down things that may seem technical or difficult to understand. She had also read a few short pieces I had written.

After a little back and forth, I admitted that I could at least attempt it. 🤭

The conversation reminded me of something I had watched just the day before: the Dunning-Kruger effect.

I had come across it before, back in school, but this time it finally made sense to me. And apparently, there is a very interesting backstory to it.

Back in 1995, a man called McArthur Wheeler robbed two banks in Pittsburgh without wearing a mask.

Imagine the guts. 😂

But what made the story especially strange was that he expected he would get away with it, and the reason he thought so. Wheeler had been told that if he rubbed lemon juice on his face, security cameras would not be able to capture him.

Why?

Because lemon juice can be used as invisible ink.

Somehow, he had taken that piece of information and concluded that it would work the same way on his face.

When the police arrested him and showed him the security footage, he was genuinely confused. He reportedly kept insisting, “But I used the juice.”

Honestly, it sounds almost too ridiculous to be true.

But it points to something very human.

The Dunning-Kruger effect is often used to describe what can happen when people have limited knowledge in an area but feel very confident in what they know. Not because they are necessarily delusional, but because they may not yet know enough to see the full shape of what they do not know.

Sometimes, the little bit we know can make us feel like we have seen the whole picture. Then, as we learn more, we begin to see the complexity. We notice the things we had not considered. We see the risks, the moving parts and the different ways things could go wrong.

And suddenly, we become more cautious.

Upon reflection, I can see this in my own life. There are things I knew just enough about to feel very sure of myself. I had a few facts, a few ideas and maybe one or two good experiences. That was enough for me to think, “Yes, I have this under control.”

Then there are things I have spent more time learning about. And strangely, those are the things that can make me pause more. I ask more questions. I become less eager to announce that I have figured everything out.

Maybe that is what happened yesterday.

Maybe I was not simply being fearful. Maybe I could now see more of the weight, uncertainty and work involved in that old dream than I could see as a child.

But then again, maybe I was allowing the complexity to make me forget what I already knew I could do.

And that is where this becomes worth thinking about.

There are things we jump at because we do not know enough to be cautious. There are also things we avoid because we know enough to see the difficulty, but not enough to trust that we can learn our way through it.

One thing I have found particularly powerful in my L&D journey is realising that learning is not always about giving people new information. Sometimes, it is about helping people take an honest look at what they already know, what they do not know and what they need to do next.

What do I know?

What can I do?

Where do I need support?

What have I not considered yet?

That kind of self-awareness can save us from confidently using the juice, only to discover later that the camera was watching the whole time.

So today, I have a question for all of us.

When you are hesitating, ask yourself: *Do I genuinely need to learn more, or am I underestimating what I already know?*

And when you are very eager to act, ask yourself: *Have I really understood this situation, or have I only understood just enough to feel confident?*

Anyway, these are my musings for today.

Look back on your life. Share a moment when you were very confident, only to realise later that you had barely scratched the surface. Or perhaps the opposite: a time when you knew more than you gave yourself credit for.

I am interested in hearing your thoughts.

Cheers.
