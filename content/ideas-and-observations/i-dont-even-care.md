---
title: I Don’t Even Care
description: A three-year-old, a lost cheat code and a reminder that people are not buttons.
---

Kids are a delight. 😀

Even though it may not always feel like that. In fact, most of the time, it does not.

An interaction with my three-year-old nephew earlier today left me frustrated, surprised and almost reeling with laughter. But before I get into it, let me give you a little backstory. Something that happened about two months ago, when I thought I had finally cracked him. I thought I had figured him out.

He had come to spend the vacation with us. One day, he was refusing to do something I wanted him to do, and in the middle of my frustration, I said, “You are not my friend.”

To my surprise, it worked.

This happened a few more times afterwards, and I began to notice a pattern. He would get upset. Then he would get sad. Then he would calm down. Then he would do whatever I had asked him to do.

Afterwards, he would come back and ask, “Am I your friend?”

And of course, I would say yes. Then all would be right with the world. 😌

As unseemly as this may sound, it worked so many times that I happily announced to my sister that I had figured him out. I had found the golden ticket.

Fast-forward to earlier today. He came by, and I was trying to get him to brush his teeth. No matter what I said, this boy would not budge.

So I decided to pull out my golden ticket.

“You are not my friend,” I said, with the self-assured confidence of someone who believed that, in a few seconds, he would pick up his brush and follow me.

This small boy looked at me, smiled and, in the sweetest sing-song manner, said, “I don’t even care.”

Imagine how that must have felt. 😅

I was surprised. I found it funny. At the same time, my feelings were hurt. I could not believe he had said that to me.

“I don’t even care.”

Wow.

It got me thinking about how sometimes we find something that works and somehow begin to see it as a permanent solution. It could be a phrase, a reward, a hack, a shortcut, or a way of getting people to cooperate. Once it works a few times, we begin to treat it like the golden ticket we have always been looking for.

Now, I did not expect that telling my nephew I was not his friend would work forever. But I expected it to work for at least another year. Maybe two or three. That could have been nice.

Instead, in the space of two months, he did not even care that I was not his friend.

People are not buttons. Even kids.

And yes, of course, I know this is not a new discovery. But indulge me.

You cannot keep pressing the same spots and expect the same response every single time. This shows up in parenting, of course, but also in leadership, teaching, friendships, work and so many other areas of life.

Sometimes we discover a way to get people moving: pressure, praise, fear, urgency, a promise, a title or a deadline. And when it works, we are tempted to keep using it.

But people change. Context changes. What once felt motivating can begin to feel manipulative, tiring or meaningless. And when it stops working, we are left feeling frustrated and wondering what happened.

Maybe the better question is not, “How do I get this person to do what I want?”

Maybe it is, “What does this person need from me right now?”

Sometimes it is clarity. Sometimes it is encouragement. Sometimes it is a reason. Sometimes it is simply a little more patience. Sometimes you have to take a breath, allow people to calm down and return to the conversation when they are ready.

And sometimes, it probably is just not going to work.

In the case of my nephew, I honestly do not know.

I have actually decided that perhaps I need to read a few books on parenting. Kids are a delight, yes, but God, they can be frustrating. They can be exhausting. 😮‍💨 They can push all your buttons in ways that leave you drained and wondering what exactly just happened.

Yes, there may not be strategies that work every single time, as I have already established. But I want to believe there are a few things I can learn.

Anyway, these are my musings for today.

I am very interested in your thoughts. More importantly, I am interested in your recommendations on how I can get my nephew to brush his teeth and, ideally, diligently do every single thing I tell him to do for at least the next seven or eight years. Wouldn’t that be the dream? 🤭

Please help an uncle out. 🥺 I am running out of patience.
