---
title: The Leader You Follow Because You Want To
description: A leadership question that made me think about the different versions of ourselves that emerge at work.
date: 2026-08-09
---

Earlier today, I was reviewing a leadership programme for one of our client organisations when one question stopped me in my tracks:

*Think of a leader you followed because you genuinely wanted to. Now think of a leader you followed because you had to.*

I sat with it for a minute, finished reviewing the programme, replied to a few emails, joined another meeting and got on with my day.

But the question would not leave me alone.

What surprised me was not how quickly the names came to mind. It was everything that came with them.

I was not just remembering leaders. I was remembering the different versions of myself that showed up around them.

The conversations I was willing to have. The ideas I felt comfortable sharing. The risks I was willing to take. The ownership I naturally assumed.

And, in some cases, the parts of myself I quietly held back.

That got me thinking.

We spend a lot of time talking about what makes a good leader. We also spend a lot of time talking about what makes a good employee.

But perhaps there is another variable we do not talk about enough: the environment.

What if the environment changes the kind of follower we become?

I am not suggesting that people are not responsible for how they show up at work. Neither am I suggesting that leaders are responsible for every behaviour on their teams.

But I do wonder whether the environments people create influence more than we realise.

Whether they make it easier for people to speak up, take initiative, ask questions, admit mistakes, challenge ideas respectfully or simply bring more of themselves to the work.

And whether, in other environments, people learn to become careful. Quiet. Defensive. Detached. Just interested enough to get through the day.

It is a question I am still mulling over.

Have you ever noticed yourself becoming a different version of yourself under different leaders? What do you think made the difference?
