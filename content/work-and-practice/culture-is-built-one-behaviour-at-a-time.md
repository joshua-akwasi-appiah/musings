---
title: Culture Is Built One Behaviour at a Time
description: Why the learning-design conversation should begin with how people need to show up, not only what they need to know.
date: 2026-08-09
---

After thinking about the kind of follower different environments can create, I found myself returning to another question:

*How are those environments created in the first place?*

One thing I have come to appreciate about designing learning experiences is that they rarely begin with content.

They begin with behaviour.

Before asking, “What do people need to know?”, I think we first have to ask, “How do we want people to show up?”

Do we want people to take ownership? Surface problems early? Challenge ideas respectfully? Follow through on commitments? Support one another? Raise their hands when they need help?

Those behaviours do not emerge by accident.

Neither do the environments that make those behaviours possible.

They are shaped by what leaders model, tolerate and reward. But they are also shaped by what everyone else accepts, reinforces or chooses to challenge.

A leader may say, “We value openness.” But if people are punished for raising difficult issues, everyone learns something else.

A team may say, “We value ownership.” But if every decision has to wait for one person’s approval, people learn to wait.

A company may say, “We value learning.” But if there is no room to practise, make mistakes or ask questions, people learn that learning is only something to complete.

Culture is not something that simply exists. It is something that is quietly built, one behaviour at a time.

That is one reason learning design matters to me. It is not only about helping people build new skills. It is also about helping organisations become more intentional about the behaviours they cultivate.

Because, eventually, those behaviours become the environment everyone experiences.
